Where the leak usually shows up

The business type changes. The failure pattern usually does not.

Owners usually feel the problem as missed momentum and avoidable drag before they describe it as a systems issue. The audit is meant to inspect the live path, not just the symptom the team complains about most.

Response lag

Intent cools off while the business decides who owns the next step.

Calls, forms, consult requests, or estimate leads sit too long before someone clearly picks them up, so the highest-intent moment passes quietly.

Follow-up drift

The first touch happens, then the rhythm depends on memory.

Teams believe they followed up, but the real process lives in inboxes, reminders, and whoever happened to remember later.

Handoff friction

Context gets lost between sales, intake, admin, dispatch, or service.

Information drops out at the handoff, which slows execution down and forces the next person to reconstruct what should already be obvious.

Admin rework

The team keeps rebuilding the same updates by hand every week.

Reports, reminders, routing updates, and status nudges get recreated manually because no one ever tightened the operating rhythm underneath them.

Not every team has every leak. The point is to isolate the one bottleneck worth fixing first.
